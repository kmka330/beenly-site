Privacy Policy: beenly

Effective date: 2026-07-27 · Last updated: 2026-08-10 · Contact: kmka330@pm.me

Short version

beenly keeps your travel data on your device. There is no server, there are no accounts, and we do not sell or share your data. There is no backend to send it to. The only things that leave your device are: (1) anonymous crash reports, so we can fix bugs, and (2) a handful of anonymous, opt-out-able usage counters. Neither ever includes which countries you have marked, your notes, or anything else that identifies you or your travel history.

Who we are

beenly is developed and published by an individual developer. For any privacy question, correction, or complaint, write to kmka330@pm.me, the same address listed as the developer contact on Google Play.

What data the app has, and where it lives

Your marked countries (status, optional visit date, optional notes) and your app settings are stored only in a local database on your device. beenly is local-first by design: it has no backend, so there is nothing to sync, no account to create, and no server-side copy of your data.

Export/import lets you write that data to a file and read it back, entirely under your control. The app never uploads it anywhere on your behalf; the exported file goes wherever you send it via the Android share sheet.

The app never accesses your contacts or any other personal data store. The one exception: if you use the Share cards feature (beenly Pro) to create a map/stats image and tap Save, the app writes that newly generated image to your device's Photos — it only ever writes an image it just created; it never reads, lists, or otherwise accesses any photo or video already on your device.

Location (optional, off by default): if you turn on Settings → Privacy → "Nudge me to mark countries", the app asks Android for approximate location access — that's the only kind of location access beenly ever requests. (Android's permission dialog may still show a "Precise" option next to "Approximate"; that's because the location library beenly uses declares support for precise location in the app's permissions, but beenly's own code never asks for it and never uses it.) While this is on, it checks your approximate location once each time you open the app (at most once every 12 hours) to see whether you're somewhere you haven't marked yet, and shows a one-time suggestion to mark it. That location value is held in memory for a moment to work out which country you're in, then discarded immediately — the location itself (your coordinates) is never written to storage, never included in an export, never sent over the network, and never attached to a crash report or a usage event.

If you dismiss a suggestion instead of marking it, the app remembers only which country you dismissed and until when — stored locally on your device so it doesn't ask about that same country again for a while. That one country id is the only thing this feature ever stores; it is never included in an export, never sent over the network, and never attached to a crash report or a usage event either. This setting is off until you turn it on, and the whole feature works fully offline (no location is ever sent to us or to Google's geocoding services — resolution happens on-device against a bundled offline map dataset).

Crash reporting (Sentry)

beenly uses Sentry to catch crashes and errors so they can be fixed. When the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, a report is sent containing: the error type and message, a stack trace, device/OS/app-version info, and a small number of non-personal breadcrumbs (e.g. "user navigated from map to passport").

Never included: which countries you have marked or their status, your notes, your visit dates, your device's location, or anything that identifies you personally. This is enforced two ways: the app never attaches place data to crash reports in the first place, and every outgoing report additionally passes through a filter that strips any field that looks like place or location data.

Legal basis (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)): legitimate interest in keeping the app working. Because crash reporting is the minimum needed to ship a reliable app, not a marketing or analytics tool, it stays on regardless of the usage-stats opt-out below.

Sentry acts as a data processor. Its own privacy terms are at https://sentry.io/privacy/.

Anonymous usage events (opt-out)

Separately from crash reports, the app can record that one of exactly fourteen things happened. This is the complete list. There is no other event, and the app cannot be made to send an arbitrary one (this is enforced in code, not just in policy):

EventWhat it tells us
app_first_openthe app was opened for the first time
country_markeda country's status changed: only whether it became visited / want-to-go / lived; never which country
passport_viewedthe Passport tab was opened
export_donean export finished
import_donean import finished
badge_earneda milestone badge was earned: only which badge (e.g. "10 countries"); never which country(ies) earned it
paywall_viewedthe beenly Pro screen was opened — no payload at all
purchase_starteda purchase was started: only whether it's a subscription or a one-time (lifetime) purchase; never which specific plan (e.g. monthly vs. yearly), a price, or a currency
purchase_completeda purchase finished successfully: only the same subscription/one-time distinction; never which specific plan, a price, or a currency
restore_completeda "restore purchases" check finished: only whether an active purchase was found; never which product
region_markeda region's status changed (beenly Pro): only whether it became visited / want-to-go / lived; never which region or which country it belongs to
goal_completedan exploration goal was reached: only the target country count (e.g. "5"); never which country(ies) counted toward it, never the baseline, never the deadline
share_card_createda share card image was created (beenly Pro): only which of the three templates; never any stats value, place, or continent name shown on it, and never which aspect ratio was picked
wrapped_viewedthe yearly "Travel Wrapped" recap was opened (beenly Pro teaser): only the recapped year (e.g. "2026"); never a country count, continent name, month, or which slide was reached

None of these carry a place id, place name, note text, date, price, currency, or any other content. No user id, email, or other personal identifier is attached.

This is opt-out. The setting is on by default; turning it off in Settings → Privacy stops all fourteen events immediately and does not affect crash reporting.

Legal basis: legitimate interest, with an unconditional opt-out in the app.

Children

beenly is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal data that could identify a child. There is no account, no profile, and no user-to-user communication.

Advertising

beenly contains no advertising and no advertising SDKs. No data is shared with advertisers or data brokers.

Data retention

Local data stays on your device until you delete it or uninstall the app. Crash reports and the anonymous usage events are retained by Sentry under its default retention window (90 days) and are not linked to you.

Your rights

Because your data lives only on your device:

  • Access & portability: the Export feature gives you a complete copy of your data at any time, in a documented JSON format.
  • Deletion: uninstalling the app deletes your local data. You may also clear the app's storage from Android Settings.
  • No account, no server-side profile: there is nothing for us to look up, because we do not hold your data. It never left your device except as the scrubbed crash/usage data described above.
  • For anything concerning the crash/usage data described above, contact kmka330@pm.me and it will be handled within 30 days.

Changes to this policy

Anything that changes what data is collected (a new telemetry event, an account/sync phase, ads, or a paywall that touches personal data) requires updating this policy and the Play Data safety form in the same release. The "Last updated" date above tracks the current version.
